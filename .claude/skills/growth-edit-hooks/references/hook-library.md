# Hook Formula Library & Scoring Rubric

Supporting reference for `growth-edit-hooks`. Read this when generating the
10-hook batch (SKILL.md §11, step 5) or scoring hooks (step 6).

## Fill-in-the-blank hook formulas

Use these as starting shapes, not verbatim templates — always fill with a
specific, real example, not a placeholder-sounding one.

- "Why does [ordinary thing] cost so much?"
- "[Common belief] isn't actually what drives [outcome]."
- "[X] → [Y]: what changed?"
- "The real reason [consumer behaviour] isn't what you think."
- "You've probably noticed this. Here's the business reason why."
- "What [brand] can teach us about [commercial concept]."
- "Same product. Different proposition."
- "Would you pay £X for this?"
- "The mistake brands make when they [action]."
- "Before you [decision], ask these [X] questions."
- "I noticed something interesting about [ordinary situation]."
- "A [familiar object] just taught me something about [commercial concept]."

## Hook scoring rubric

Score every hook 1–10 on each dimension before selecting. Targets below are
the bar for "ready to build" — a hook that misses a target by more than a
point or two on any dimension needs another pass, not a pass-through.

| Dimension | Question | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Scroll-stop | Would someone pause on Slide 1? | 8+/10 |
| Curiosity | Is there a genuine unanswered question? | 8+/10 |
| Specificity | Does it feel concrete rather than generic? | 8+/10 |
| Relevance | Does the audience immediately understand why it matters? | 8+/10 |
| Shareability | Would someone send it to another person? | 7+/10 |
| Follow potential | Does it make the account feel worth following? | 8+/10 |
| Credibility | Can the claim be supported? | 9+/10 |
| Originality | Does it avoid sounding like generic business content? | 8+/10 |
| Brand fit | Does it feel unmistakably The Growth Edit? | 9+/10 |

Select the strongest hook by curiosity × credibility × relevance × brand
fit — not by scroll-stop alone. A hook that scores high on scroll-stop but
low on credibility or brand fit is a worse pick than a slightly quieter hook
that nails all four.
