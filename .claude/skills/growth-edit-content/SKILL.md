# The Growth Edit — Content Guidelines

Standing rules for any content written for The Growth Edit's Instagram.

## Brand story: the three-step structure

Every piece of content should map to this arc:

**WHAT WE LOOK AT → THE QUESTION WE ASK → HOW WE THINK**

1. **What we look at** — the everyday thing. Coffee, Bond, champagne, a fashion trend, an app, a queue outside a restaurant. Anything.
2. **The question we ask** — the "but why..." moment. Why does this cost what it costs? Why did this brand win? Why do we accept this?
3. **How we think** — the underlying lens: value, price, brand, experience, perception, scarcity, story, status.

"Everything in between" is the open umbrella covering step 1 — it is deliberately NOT limited to finance, pricing, or business. Future topics can be pop culture, human behaviour, products, brands, trends, or anything a curious general reader would stop scrolling for.

## The clarity rule (new)

Every fact, statistic, or explanation must be understandable by someone with **zero background in business, finance, or marketing** — a smart friend, not a colleague.

- If a sentence needs a business degree to parse, rewrite it in plain language.
- Explain mechanisms the way you'd explain them out loud to a curious friend, not the way you'd write them in a strategy deck.
- Simplicity and interest are not in tension — the goal is "that's actually really interesting," not "that sounds impressive." If a draft impresses but doesn't clarify, cut it back.
- Never assume the reader knows a term (markup, margin, positioning, etc.) without a plain-English translation nearby, unless the term itself is the fun fact.
- Test: could someone explain this fact correctly to another person after reading it once? If not, simplify further.

This applies even when the underlying idea is sophisticated — the *idea* can be smart; the *sentence* should never require effort.

## Voice & tone

- Natural British English.
- Dry, elegant humour — light, witty, understated. Never self-deprecating, never corporate, never "LinkedIn guru" (no "unlock your potential").
- Short sentences. One clean beat per slide — don't stack multiple jokes or ideas in one line.
- Should read like something a smart, curious person would screenshot and send to a friend — not like an ad, and not like a lecture.

## Visual identity

- **Feel:** quiet luxury, minimalist, editorial, "modern London" — a luxury magazine meets a London lifestyle editorial, not a consulting firm.
- **Palette:** warm ivory, soft beige, cream, taupe, light stone, espresso brown, dark charcoal, with wine (`#6B2430`) as the single reserved accent colour. Subtle tonal variation, not multiple hues.
- **Typography:** serif display for headlines, clean sans for body, generous whitespace, consistent margins and hierarchy slide to slide.
- **Avoid:** corporate blue, loud colours, decorative icons, generic stock photography, cheap AI imagery, excessive bolding, crowded slides, text position jumping between slides.

## Content philosophy — hook strategy

Optimise every post for: curiosity → emotional reaction → reveal → "I never thought about it that way" → share/save.

- **Start with something people already care about — not something you want to teach.** Coffee, champagne, fashion, supermarkets, travel, luxury, everyday products, weird prices, cultural habits, surprising facts.
- **The hook should create an information gap.** Make people think "Wait… why?", "How is that possible?" or "I never knew that." Curiosity earns the first 2 seconds. The insight earns the follow.
- **Never lead with the lesson.** Avoid "Here's a pricing strategy…" — instead "Why does this coffee cost £100?" The pricing lesson comes later.
- **Make the content feel like entertainment with a smart payoff.** The viewer should feel they discovered something interesting — not sat through a business lecture.
- **Use familiar things as the entry point, unexpected things as the twist.** Ordinary product → strange fact → unexpected explanation → bigger idea.
- **Build hooks around tension, contrast and disbelief.** e.g. "It's the same coffee. So why does one cost 20x more?" / "Why does Japan buy most of this coffee?" / "This £5 drink starts with something worth about £1." / "Champagne has a problem nobody is talking about."
- **Delay the commercial/psychological insight until the audience is invested.** The final reveal can be: value, pricing, scarcity, branding, perception, positioning, distribution, consumer psychology or experience.
- **Make the insight bigger than business.** The best posts should leave someone thinking "That explains why luxury works," "That's why I'm willing to pay more," or "I'm going to notice this everywhere now."
- **Optimise for shares and follows, not just likes.** Prioritise ideas that make people want to send it to a friend, save it for later, comment with their opinion, or follow because they want the next surprising story.

### The formula

```
FAMILIAR THING
      ↓
SURPRISING FACT
      ↓
  "WAIT, WHY?"
      ↓
UNEXPECTED STORY
      ↓
  THE TWIST
      ↓
THE GROWTH EDIT INSIGHT
```

The brand should feel like: *"I came for the £100 coffee. I stayed because now I understand why people pay for things."*

The Growth Edit should never feel like a finance account disguised as lifestyle content. It should feel like a smart, stylish page that makes you see ordinary things differently — with commercial thinking hidden underneath.

### North-star test for every post

Would someone who knows absolutely nothing about pricing, strategy or business still stop scrolling?

- If yes → publish.
- If the hook requires knowing what "value creation" or "price architecture" means → rewrite the hook.

## Data & accuracy standards

- All statistics must be real, verifiable, and (where relevant) UK-specific.
- Prefer percentage-based framing over raw figures where it clarifies commercial impact.
- Verify stats before copy is locked — never approximate or invent a number.

## Workflow

1. Copy first, design second — lock copy before any visual work begins.
2. Every slide/post gets checked against the clarity rule above before it's considered final.
3. Template-first in Canva: establish and name templates, then duplicate and populate for each new post.
4. Iterative feedback loops — specific, directional corrections rather than open-ended rewrites.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Fits the three-step arc (what we look at → question → how we think)
- [ ] Hook is a familiar thing + surprising fact — never the lesson, never a business framing
- [ ] Passes the north-star test: would someone with zero interest in business still stop scrolling?
- [ ] Insight is delayed until the audience is invested, and lands bigger than "business"
- [ ] Every fact/stat is explainable by someone with no business background
- [ ] Business insight (if present) lands as payoff, not premise
- [ ] All stats real, verifiable, percentage-framed where relevant
- [ ] Tone: dry British humour, no self-deprecation, no corporate language
- [ ] Visual consistency across slides (typography, margins, positioning, rhythm)
- [ ] No excessive bolding, no crowded slides
- [ ] Final slide is a clean, quotable one-liner
